Chapter one

A blank wall, a long search

Every AURELLE piece goes back to one ordinary problem: a newly painted wall and nothing worth hanging on it. We looked at flat-pack prints and factory-made showpieces. They were fine, but none of them felt like anyone had made them with care.

So we started asking a different question: who makes the beautiful things we remember from our grandparents’ homes? The brass diya on the pooja shelf, the dhurrie by the bed, the carved wooden tray that came out for guests.

The answer led us to workshops, not warehouses. AURELLE is our way of bringing those makers’ work into modern homes, with honest descriptions, fair prices and packaging that arrives in one piece.

How a piece is made

From the workshop to your wall

Four steps, none of them rushed.

01

Cast & shape

Brass swans, diyas and urlis begin as molten metal poured into moulds, then are filed, turned and hand-polished until they glow. No two pieces finish exactly alike.

02

Weave & stitch

Cushion covers, dhurries and throws come off wooden handlooms where a weaver works the pattern row by row. The slight irregularities you see are the proof that a person made it.

03

Turn, glaze & finish

Vases and planters are thrown, ribbed or fluted by hand, left to dry slowly and fired. Wall plates are painted one at a time, which is why our sets always look collected rather than matched.

04

Pack & send

Fragile pieces are wrapped in paper, nested in straw and boxed twice. Before an order leaves, someone checks it against your list and writes your name on the box.

Where the inspiration comes from

Craft traditions we love

Indian homes have a long history of making beautiful, useful things by hand. These are some of the traditions our pieces draw from.

Moradabad brass

Known for generations as the “Brass City” of Uttar Pradesh, famous for engraved and cast metalwork.

Jaipur dhurries & block prints

Flat-woven cotton rugs and hand-blocked textiles in warm, geometric patterns.

Saharanpur woodwork

Carved and turned wood furniture and trays, often finished in deep walnut tones.

Cane & seagrass weaving

Baskets, pendant shades and poufs woven from natural fibres that soften light and texture.
